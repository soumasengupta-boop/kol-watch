This week

A quiet, low-volume week (38 relevant posts vs 82 prior, only 9 of 91 KOLs active) dominated by Eric Topol's AI, genomics and policy commentary, with HORIZON/Lp(a) follow-up and a JACC LAAC focus issue the main cardiology threads.

Topol: AI diagnosis and opportunistic AI

Eric Topol posted two AI diagnosis cases (a 37-year-old woman with 10 years of abdominal pain, and a diagnosis missed for decades). He also noted that >70 million US chest X-rays a year do not report osteoporotic fracture risk, calling this a missed 'opportunistic AI' era. He also promoted a conversation with Ethan Mollick and a clip on AI making surgery safer.

Risk prediction: pharmacogenomics and plasma proteins

Topol highlighted that everyone carries at least one DNA variant affecting medication response, and that few people know theirs. He also shared a Nature Metabolism paper reporting that plasma proteins predict type 2 diabetes, liver, kidney and heart disease better than current metrics. Risk prediction was the fastest-rising named topic (5 posts vs 1).

HORIZON and Lp(a) follow-up after AHA

Kausik Ray posted the EAS statement on the HORIZON results and what they mean for Lp(a). C. Michael Gibson posted a discussion with Pam Taub on HORIZON's implications for prevention, guidelines and practice. Deepak Bhatt shared a recap of major AHA trials. No results were stated in these posts.

JACC LAAC focus issue drives antithrombotic posts

JACC posted several items from its LAAC issue. In OPTION, after AF ablation, LAAC and oral anticoagulation had similar 36-month death, stroke or embolism in both sexes. Bleeding fell more in men, driven by non-major bleeding (11.1% vs 3.9%). ASAP-TOO (LAAC vs SAPT/no antithrombotic) was underpowered from poor accrual, with no significant difference in ischemic stroke/systemic embolism. A review of 8 trials in >7,000 AF patients stressed patient selection and shared decisions.

Gibson's COMPLETE/COMPLETE-2 Grand Rounds

Gibson's repeated Baim Grand Rounds posts accounted for the COMPLETE and COMPLETE-2 trial mentions. Shamir Mehta presented 'Resolving the Anatomy vs. Physiology Debate in STEMI Multivessel Disease Revascularization', and the recording was posted. Gibson also covered asundexian and ischemic stroke prevention (ESOC data), HFpEF recognition, and a real-world ATTR-CM study. The study associated acoramidis with an 18% lower risk of the composite outcome than tafamidis.

Equity, health policy and prevention themes

JACC reported that CV mortality varied more among 3,110 US counties than among states. It also argued that race in clinical equations should depend on purpose, evidence and consequences. Topol criticized the US failure to prevent a disease considered eliminated 26 years ago, and said the NIH budget is flat over 20+ years in inflation-adjusted terms. He also discussed Jo Chikwe on diversity in cardiac surgery.
